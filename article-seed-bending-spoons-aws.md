## The argument in full (from session — use verbatim as draft spine)

Bending Spoons bought WeTransfer. Bending Spoons bought Smash.
Two products. Same acquirer. Same playbook: gut the free tier, harvest the data harder, upsell aggressively.

AWS provides the storage for both.

So the file goes: sender's laptop → AWS → receiver's laptop, with Smash logging both ends the entire time. WeTransfer the same. Their own privacy policies confirm this — Amazon Web Services listed as infrastructure provider in both.

One subpoena to Amazon. The door opens on both.

That is not a Refueler claim. That is their own infrastructure disclosure.

## Structure

1. **The acquisition paragraph.** Bending Spoons bought both. State it flatly. No editorialising. Link to the announcements.
2. **The infrastructure paragraph.** Both run on AWS. Cite their own privacy policies — Article 5 of Smash's policy names AWS explicitly. Dropbox names AWS. These are not accusations. These are their own published disclosures.
3. **The legal mechanics.** A US federal request to Amazon — under the CLOUD Act — does not require notification to the service or the user. The threshold is lower than a UK court order. The provider is not necessarily in the chain of notification.
4. **The one-subpoena line.** Short paragraph. "One legal request to one provider. The door opens on both services simultaneously. The files were always in the same building."
5. **What Share does differently.** Not a feature list. One sentence: the server holds encrypted noise and chunk timestamps. The key was in the URL fragment. It was never transmitted. There is nothing in the building worth opening the door for.
6. **Close.** Founder voice. One or two sentences. Not a CTA. A statement.

- Bending Spoons funding sources: "Amazon-funded" stated conversationally in session — verify before including in published piece. May be PE-backed, not Amazon directly. The AWS infrastructure point is the stronger and verifiable claim.
